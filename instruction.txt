VISTA – Field Inventory Management System

Act as a Senior Full-Stack Developer and Software Architect.

I have an existing Field Inventory Management System named VISTA. The system has three primary roles:

Super Admin

Inventory Manager

Floor Staff

The system manages organizational inventory across multiple warehouses, facilities, storage locations, and field operations.

This is a single-organization system, not a multi-tenant application.

Your responsibility is to audit, repair, and complete the existing application. Make every page, route, form, field, button, API, dashboard, assignment, and inventory workflow fully functional.

1. Super Admin – Organization-Wide Management

The Super Admin must be able to:

Manage all warehouses and storage facilities.

Create, update, activate, and deactivate warehouses.

Create and manage Inventory Manager accounts.

Assign Managers to their respective warehouses.

Reassign warehouses between Managers.

Manage Floor Staff accounts and role permissions.

View all users and their assignments.

Manage the master inventory catalog.

Manage product/item categories, SKUs, units, and barcodes.

Monitor inventory across all warehouses.

View warehouse-wise stock and inventory movements.

Monitor stock shortages, damaged items, and discrepancies.

View global reports, audit logs, and operational activities.

2. Inventory Manager – Warehouse Management

Each Manager should only manage their assigned warehouse or warehouses.

Managers must be able to:

View and manage their assigned warehouse details.

View warehouse inventory and stock levels.

Manage assigned Floor Staff.

Assign staff to storage areas, sections, zones, and bins.

Assign shifts and daily operational tasks.

Monitor incoming and outgoing inventory.

Review stock requests submitted by staff.

Approve or reject stock requests.

Review stock adjustments and physical count discrepancies.

Monitor damaged, missing, and expired items where applicable.

Manage warehouse receiving, storage, picking, issuing, and dispatch operations.

Manage authorized stock transfers.

Generate warehouse-specific reports.

Managers must not access or modify another Manager's warehouses or staff without authorization.

3. Floor Staff – Field Operations

Floor Staff should perform actual inventory operations within their assigned warehouse and operational area.

They must be able to:

View their assigned warehouse and storage location.

View assigned inventory and available stock.

Receive incoming inventory.

Record item quantities and relevant identification details.

Scan item barcodes where supported.

Store items in assigned locations.

Pick and issue inventory against approved requests.

Submit stock replenishment requests.

Perform physical stock counts.

Report missing, damaged, or incorrect inventory.

Record returned items.

Submit purchase or procurement records where authorized.

Create and track transfer requests.

Confirm physical receipt of transferred inventory where authorized.

View assigned tasks and request statuses.

Staff must not approve their own stock requests or make unauthorized stock adjustments.

4. Core Inventory Workflows

Make the following workflows fully functional and connected:

Inventory receiving and stock entry.

Warehouse storage and bin allocation.

Stock lookup and availability.

Stock requests and replenishment approvals.

Picking, issuing, and dispatch.

Physical stock counting.

Stock discrepancies and adjustments.

Damaged and missing inventory reporting.

Inventory returns.

Procurement and purchase recording.

Inter-warehouse stock transfers.

Inventory reports and audit trails.

Adapt these workflows to the actual inventory requirements of the existing project. Do not introduce medical, clinical, pharmaceutical, ward, or patient-related functionality.

5. Organizational Relationships

Implement the following hierarchy:

Super Admin
├── Warehouse A → Manager A → Assigned Staff
├── Warehouse B → Manager B → Assigned Staff
└── Warehouse C → Manager C → Assigned Staff

Support multiple Managers per warehouse if operationally required and multiple warehouse assignments per Manager where authorized.

Keep role, warehouse assignment, staff supervision, and operational-area assignment separate.

6. Important Implementation Requirements

Inspect the existing codebase before making changes.

Review every route and every existing form.

Connect every frontend field to the correct backend functionality.

Ensure all updates persist in the actual database.

Use real database data rather than static or dummy data.

Enforce role-based and warehouse-based authorization on the server.

Keep inventory balances and stock movement records consistent.

Require authorization for sensitive stock adjustments.

Prevent unauthorized stock issuance and negative inventory.

Preserve the existing application architecture and working features.

Test each workflow using separate Super Admin, Manager, and Floor Staff accounts.

Do not rebuild the application from scratch.

First audit the existing implementation, identify missing functionality, and then implement and test each feature systematically.
